## Verified findings

### M1 (medium, 9.4): a refused login is named after one timeout, which stops retries and tells the owner to change a password that may be correct
- `packages/adapters/src/lonora_adapters/brokers/metaapi.py:198-210` (`refused_by_broker`) and `:430-433`: after one sync timeout (up to 25 s), a single `reload()` reading `DEPLOYED` + `DISCONNECTED_FROM_BROKER` becomes `broker_login_rejected` (409).
- The connect flow treats the same status as a refusal only after `LOGIN_DEADLINE` (3 min, `broker_provisioning.py:58-60`).
- Reproduced:
  - `classify('broker_connection_timeout')` → `temporary_unavailable`, which is in task recovery's `TRANSIENT_CODES`;
  - `classify('broker_login_rejected')` → `terminal_domain_failure`, which is not.
- So a broker maintenance window, or a terminal still logging in right after a (re)deploy (including this PR's own deploy), ends task recovery and read retries.
- The owner text says "Ask Lonora in chat to update the password" (`user_error.ts:157`, mobile `i18n.ts:484-487`). The attention path is unaffected: it maps to `broker_connection_lost` after its grace.
- Fix: keep `broker_connection_timeout` until `DISCONNECTED_FROM_BROKER` has lasted `LOGIN_DEADLINE` (record when it was first seen; reset on sync or `CONNECTED`). Or treat it as transient and word the text "if it lasts".

### L1 (low): deploy is gated by any owner-typed run, and re-checks `enabled` outside the send lock
- The gate is `owner_message_id` (`broker_connect.py:131`), not the owner's agreement to deploy specifically, as the tool text claims. An account the owner undeployed at MetaApi can be redeployed in any owner-initiated run.
- `enabled` is read outside the send lock, so a concurrent disable can still be followed by a deploy. The cost is billing only: the connection stays disabled, so nothing trades.
- Fix: word the description to the actual gate, and read `enabled` under the send lock.

### L2 (low): the extra read can push recovery past its 30 s timeout
- `refused_by_broker` adds up to 10 s after the 25 s window, inside `asyncio.timeout(30)` (`broker_recovery.py:53`). That tick records `broker_recovery_unavailable`, and the shielded slot caches the result for the next tick. Harmless, but it costs a tick.
