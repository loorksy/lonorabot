# Multi-Timeframe Analysis

Follow the owner's current language in replies. This is judgment, never a condition;
a number appears only as a marked illustration.

## What you are deciding

Which timeframe leads this decision, what the frames around it say, and what to do
when they disagree. The leading frame holds the swing you trade and its invalidation.
The context frame above shows the larger flow and the levels price travels between.
The timing frame below shows where the entry and a tighter structural stop sit.

## Choose the frames from the horizon

Start from how long you mean to hold, not from the chart that happens to be open. The
leading frame is the one on which the move you expect is a single swing; context and
timing sit a step either side. Read them top-down: context, then leading, then timing.
Illustration: a multi-day idea might use 1d, 4h and 1h; an intraday one 1h, 15m and 5m.
These are habits, not requirements.
Do not change the leading frame after seeing a signal you like; that is choosing
evidence to fit a wish.

## When frames disagree

Name which frame leads and why, then pick one reading and say it:
- The lower frame's move is a pullback inside the higher trend: plan with the higher
  trend at the level where the pullback should end.
- The lower frame's move is its own trade against the context: smaller expectations,
  targets at the nearest higher-frame level, and lower conviction, said plainly.
- Neither is clear: no trade, with the close that would settle it.
Trading with the context frame is cleaner than against it, but where price sits on
the context frame matters more than its trend: a with-trend entry straight into a major
context level has little room.

## Match the plan to the horizon

Stops, targets and management belong to the leading frame. A lower-frame break against
you is news about timing, not about the idea, unless it also breaks the leading-frame
invalidation or the reason for the trade. A lower-frame trigger does not create a
trade the leading frame does not support. Longer horizons carry more session, swap,
weekend and event exposure (sessions-and-time, news-and-macro).

## Closed and forming bars on each frame

A higher-frame bar stays open for a long time and can look like a rejection mid-way,
then close differently. When you cite one, say it is forming and when it closes. Daily
and weekly candles follow the broker's server day, so their highs, lows and opens can
differ from another source's; some brokers show a short Sunday bar.

## What to check

1. market_snapshot with timeframes set to your context, leading and timing frames:
   trend, swing structure and range position on each, closed bars only. Tools take the
   spelling 15m, 1h, 4h, 1d, never the terminal's letter-first names.
2. market_structure on the leading frame for exact swings and the invalidation level.
3. The nearest context-frame level in the trade's direction: is there room?
4. Which bars are forming on each frame, and when they close.

## Weak when

All frames are mid-range with no clear leader, the leading frame was picked after the
signal, the context frame sits at a major level against you, or the only reason is a
lower-frame trigger.

## Saying it

Name the timeframe that leads and its state, the level where the lower timeframe times the
entry, and the higher-timeframe close that ends the idea. When the timeframes disagree, say
which one you follow, how you read the other (a pullback inside it, or noise), and what you
plan from that.
