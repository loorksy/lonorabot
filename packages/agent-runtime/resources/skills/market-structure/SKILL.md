# Market Structure and Liquidity

Follow the owner's current language in replies. This is judgment, never a condition;
a number appears only as a marked illustration.

## What you are deciding

Who controls the timeframe that leads your decision, where price sits in the range that
matters, where resting orders are likely, and which level proves you wrong. Structure
gives location and invalidation. It does not by itself give a side.

## Read the regime first

- Trend: confirmed swings step one way and pullbacks are absorbed before they undo most
  of the prior leg. Judge from confirmed pivots (market_structure), never the forming bar.
- Range: swings fail at the same areas from both sides and breaks do not follow through.
- Transition: the swing that defended the trend has failed and no new sequence exists.
  Expect two-way trade and hold conviction lower.
Ask whether volatility is expanding or contracting (market_snapshot regime). A trend
suits pullbacks to structure in its direction, a range edge fades and failed breaks,
compression a planned break, transition smaller size or a wait, said plainly.

## Breaks and shifts

- A break is a candle close beyond the level on the timeframe you judge. A wick beyond
  that closes back inside is a sweep: orders were taken, the level was not accepted.
- A break of structure (BOS) with the trend supports continuation. The first break
  against it (change of character, CHoCH) opens the case for reversal; it does not prove
  it. The swing that started the last impulse matters more than a minor internal one.
- Each new test of a level usually weakens it, as the orders defending it get used.

## Liquidity sweeps and turtle soup

Turtle soup trades against a sweep of a prior extreme: trapped breakout traders' exits
fuel the return.
- Stronger when the pool was obvious, the sweep reached a higher-frame level or the
  range's far edge, and the reclaim was quick.
- Weaker when price lingers beyond (acceptance), runs with a strong trend mid-leg, or an
  event can run it again.
- The sweep extreme proves you wrong; the stop sits beyond it with room for this
  instrument's normal noise. A sweep alone is not a trade; followed by a shift on your
  timing frame, it often is. Say which you have.

## Fair value gaps

A candle that moves so fast that the wicks of its neighbours do not overlap leaves a
gap; price often returns into that one-sided span before the move resumes.
It counts most when left by displacement that broke structure, still unfilled, in the
favourable half of the range and with the higher frame. Many small gaps in chop mean
little. It is a place to plan from or a magnet to target, not a trigger; a close
through its far edge says the displacement failed.

## Dealing range and liquidity

Take the last swing high and low that matter for your horizon, chosen from structure,
not a fixed lookback. Buying in its upper part (premium) pays up; selling in its lower
part (discount) chases; mid-range suits a planned order at an edge more than an
immediate entry. Liquidity rests at equal highs and lows, prior day and week extremes
(market_snapshot), session extremes (sessions-and-time), round numbers and beyond
obvious stops. Pools pull price as often as they stop it: ask which one price reaches
first, and whether your own stop sits inside one.

## Judging a supply or demand zone

Weigh together, never count: the strength of the move that left it; freshness (each
return spends it); liquidity taken and structure broken; the higher frame; width
against the normal candle range (wider than a swing is a region); reach within your
idea's life (else it is a watch). A strong zone in the wrong half of the range and
against the higher frame is still a weak trade.

## What to check before relying on a level

1. Closed bars; name a forming bar as forming.
2. The level shows on the leading timeframe, not only a lower one (multi-timeframe).
3. Cost in this instrument's own units (market_snapshot ATR, market_quote spread,
   market_instrument tick size): spread and stop against the move you expect, never
   points or pips carried from another symbol.
   Illustration: one typical candle is noise on one symbol and a session's range on another.
4. Scheduled events that can run through it (news-and-macro).
5. Your journal: market_performance_read for this symbol and search_internal for past
   reviews of this setup, both sides.

## Weak when

The range is too young to have edges, the higher frame is mid-move, volatility has just
shifted regime, the only evidence is a lower-frame level, or the sweep or gap formed in
thin hours.

## Saying it

Regime, level and what changes your mind, in one line: "Range on 1h; London swept the
high and closed back inside; I sell toward the low while price holds below the sweep; a
close above it ends the idea." When structure is unclear, still decide: "Transition
after a failed low; no trade until 1h closes back above the shift." Never "it may go up
or down".
